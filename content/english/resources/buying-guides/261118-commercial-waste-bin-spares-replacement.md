A broken wheel or lid does not automatically mean that an entire commercial bin must be replaced. Nor does a part that looks similar prove that repair is safe or accepted by the collector. Before ordering a fleet, agree **which exact parts can be supplied and replaced, who authorizes the work, and when the bin must be withdrawn**. If those answers are missing for a damaged bin, hold it until the owner and competent parties decide.

The image shows examples of bin configurations, not a spare-parts catalogue or a compatibility claim.

## Identify the bin before asking for a part

Record the owner, manufacturer, exact model and revision, capacity, marking or production code, lid type, wheel and brake arrangement, lifting interface, service location and collector. Photograph the complete bin and the damaged area, including relevant labels and mounting points. Record the part number and side or position if available. Capacity and colour alone are not enough; obtain the drawing or identification method from the actual manufacturer.

For a lid, ask about its profile, hinges, pin positions, lock or aperture and clearance during emptying. For a wheel or castor, confirm the assembly, mounting pattern, brake and intended position. For a damaged receiver, lifting trunnion, rim or structural body, do not assume a replacement part or a patch will restore the lifting function. The manufacturer and collector must define whether work is permitted and how the bin is accepted afterwards.

Weber's [MGB 1100 flat-lid assembly manual](https://www.w-weber.com/en/wheelie-bins-1100-l-fl/wheelie-bins-1100-l-fd-spare-parts.pdf), pages 2–6, distinguishes left and right lifting parts, lid components and braked versus unbraked wheels for that specific model. It is an example of why exact identification matters; its parts and installation values do not apply to other bins without confirmation.

## Separate hold, repair and replacement

- **Hold:** isolate a bin with an uncertain identity, a damaged lifting feature, unstable wheel or brake, structural crack, distorted rim, insecure lid or other condition affecting control. Follow the owner and site procedure; do not present it for collection while its suitability is unresolved.
- **Assess repair:** ask the owner, original manufacturer and collector whether an identified part and authorized method exist, who may carry out the work, and what checks and records are needed before release. Do not drill, weld, substitute fasteners or alter a lifting interface on the strength of this article.
- **Replace:** use the agreed withdrawal route when a safe, accepted repair cannot be demonstrated, the manufacturer or owner requires withdrawal, parts or responsibility remain unresolved, or replacement is the better supported decision. Dispose of or recycle the old bin through the owner's approved route.

UK [WISH WASTE 05, issue 1, page 9](https://www.wishforum.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/WASTE-05.pdf) links safe emptying to bin integrity and hoist engagement and discusses maintenance and replacement of faulty bins. It is UK industry guidance, not a blanket legal rule for every market. Use the applicable local requirements and the actual manufacturer's instructions.

## Compare costs only after safety and acceptance

For a repairable case, compare the same time horizon and include parts, freight, competent labour, downtime, inspection and any collector reapproval. For replacement, include the delivered bin, setup, any service interruption and agreed disposal of the old unit. Check quotes and taxes for the actual site; do not treat a low part price as the full cost.

For illustration, in one currency: parts 35 + labour 25 + downtime 10 = **70**; replacement bin 90 + delivery 15 + disposal 5 = **110**. The difference is 40 for those assumed inputs only. If either option needs extra inspections or downtime, add those costs; neither option is approved by the calculation.

## Put the spare-parts plan in the purchase order

Ask each supplier for the exact offered model and revision, parts list or identification method, replacement wheels, brakes, lids and hardware where available, expected support arrangements, ordering units and lead times. Ask which work is authorized, what tools or training and post-repair checks are required, and how model or part changes will be communicated. Confirm warranty, pricing, minimum quantities and availability for the particular order; do not assume parts are interchangeable across brands.

Agree who owns and pays for inspection, parts, repair, collection-service approval and disposal. Keep the approved configuration and repair history with the bin ID. Recheck the [collector's lift interface](/resources/buying-guides/260828-commercial-wheeled-bin-lift-interface-check/) and the [site push route](/resources/buying-guides/260904-commercial-wheeled-bin-push-route-check/) after a relevant change; a successful repair of one component does not independently approve the whole route.
