For industrial parts, the choice between solid and vented walls is a trade-off between **containing and shielding the contents, and allowing air or water to pass through**. Start with the actual parts and route, then check the walls and base separately. A vented wall does not prove an open base; a solid wall does not prove a sealed box.

The photo shows a box with openings in its walls. It does not establish its base construction, slot size, liquid retention or suitability for a particular part.

## Start with the material and route

Record part shape and smallest loose items, packaged or loose state, mass, sharp edges, oil or moisture, acceptable contamination and sensitive surfaces. Identify filling, movement, storage, washing, draining and empty return. A solid-wall candidate may be useful where small items or incidental dust need more protection. A vented-wall candidate may be worth testing where airflow or drainage matters and the goods cannot escape. Neither prevents all contamination or guarantees process performance.

Ask where openings exist in each candidate: long and short walls, floor, corners, joints, handles and drain features. A separate liner or inner pack can change containment, cleanliness, airflow and empty return; confirm who supplies it and whether it stays in place. [Congost's 1796P data sheet](https://www.congost.com/PDF/FT/EN/Congost-DS-1796P-euro-stacking-container-perforated-walls-solid-base.pdf) describes a smaller container with perforated walls and a solid base: it illustrates independent choices, not a Baoheng specification.

## Compare openings with the actual part

Ask for a drawing and measure the usable opening at the narrowest relevant point, including shape, length, moulding variation and openings at joints. Check the smallest component in every orientation it can take during filling, vibration and discharge. Powder, small pieces or flexible packaging require their own containment assessment.

For a **hypothetical** 8 × 30 mm slot and a cylindrical pin 6 mm in diameter and 20 mm long, the differences are 2 mm in width and 10 mm in length. If the pin aligns with an unobstructed slot, it could pass through; the arithmetic does not predict actual loss frequency. Reject that configuration for an uncontained pin unless an approved inner pack or other solution addresses the risk. These dimensions are examples, not dimensions of a Baoheng box.

## Check protection, water and cleanout separately

Solid panels can reduce direct exposure through that panel, but gaps, the open top, lid fit and handling still matter. A solid base is not automatically watertight, suitable for liquids or secondary containment. If liquid retention is required, obtain evidence for the exact configuration and site rules; use an appropriate approved containment system where needed.

Openings can provide a possible route for air or water, but a filled load, liner, stacked box or solid floor may block it. Specify whether drainage is needed from the goods, the box floor or both, and where water may go. [Agrico's comparison](https://agricoplastiques.com/en/folding-wall-pallet-boxes) distinguishes wall types for its own models and says liquid containment needs confirmation. Do not transfer its model claims to another box.

If cleaning is required, identify the approved method, chemicals and temperature, access to ribs and joints, drainage, drying and release criteria. Direct food contact, chemical exposure and hygiene claims require separate evidence for the actual model and jurisdiction.

## Trial the exact combination and order against it

Have responsible personnel agree sample identity, representative safe parts and fill, liner, handling route, observations and stop criteria. Check retention in the intended orientations, no trapped or damaged parts, access for cleaning and the observed water path where relevant. Check loaded stacking and forklift handling under the approved conditions, not an improvised overload. Stop for loss of contents, unstable stacks, damage or an uncontrolled leak. A trial covers only the documented configuration and conditions.

Compare each model's dimensions, floor and wall openings, load and support conditions, stacking, lid, cleaning and return separately.
