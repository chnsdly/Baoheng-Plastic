A replacement tray can have the right overall length and still be wrong for a blast-freezer rack. Its rim may not bear on the rails, its loaded base may sag into the space below, or the filled layer may obstruct the air path. **Approve the exact tray, load and rack together**, using the freezer owner's process requirements and the tray supplier's model-specific information.

This guide concerns trays holding packaged goods in a rack or trolley. If goods or process liquids touch the tray, obtain the relevant direct-contact evidence for the exact configuration and jurisdiction before approval. The pictured trays show product form only; the image does not establish rack compatibility, cold performance or food-contact approval.

## Record the installed interface

With the equipment owner, identify the freezer and rack revision, rail profile, tray orientation and the areas intended to support the tray. Measure the clear distance between inside rail faces, support width, rail length and end stops, and the vertical pitch between adjacent support levels. Record millimetres, measurement points and the tightest relevant locations. Measure the sample's outside dimensions, rim or ledge geometry, base depth, allowable dimensional tolerances and any projections that can catch during insertion.

**Illustrative geometry only:** if the gap between rail inside faces is 560 mm and the tray's outside width at that height is 600 mm, the difference is 40 mm; perfect centring gives 20 mm on each side. That is not the verified bearing width: outer edges may not be the load-bearing ledges, the rails may have different contact profiles, and tolerances or skew reduce actual engagement. The rack owner must define minimum engagement and confirm the measured loaded arrangement. Do not use these example dimensions as acceptance limits.

Ask the tray supplier for the exact model, drawing and revision, stated dimensions and tolerances, material, load and temperature limits, care instructions and any available evidence for the intended support. Unknowns remain open; neither a similar-looking tray nor a nominal size establishes suitability.

## Check the filled arrangement, not just the empty fit

Describe the packaged product, maximum tray load including packaging, contact pattern, fill height, liner, and normal temperature range. Check the loaded tray can be inserted and withdrawn without forcing, tipping or catching. Under the responsible team's approved method, inspect the contact on both rails and observe base deformation and changes in engagement at the intended temperature and duration. Do not stand under a loaded rack or overload a sample to find its limit.

Measure the usable vertical space from the highest point of the filled load to the underside or obstruction above, at the points specified by the equipment owner. Base deflection, package bulging, rail geometry and insertion clearance can change that space. A positive geometric gap alone does not prove adequate airflow or cooling performance.

FAO's [air-blast freezer guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/x5913e/x5913e01.htm) explains that tray spacing, loading and air bypass affect the path through a freezer. Its examples concern specific freezer and product arrangements; use the actual equipment manufacturer's requirements, not its example dimensions, for another line.

## Agree a controlled trial and decision

Before the trial, the freezer owner, process team, supplier and safety personnel should agree on sample identity, representative packaged load, rack positions, temperature, duration, measurements, acceptance criteria, stop conditions and decision owner. Assess manual handling and provide a controlled area. Do not infer a permitted number of cycles or a safe working load from this article.

Run an empty fit check, then a representative loaded check only under the approved procedure. Observe both side supports, insertion, withdrawal, sag, cracking, unstable packages and any contact with the tray above. Record the warmest relevant product locations and process outcomes using the operator's approved monitoring method. The process owner decides acceptable product temperature, time, airflow and quality; the tray vendor cannot approve those outcomes by quoting vent area.

Stop and isolate a tray that cracks, loses support, jams, shifts the load or exceeds an agreed clearance or deflection limit. Record the location, condition and evidence for the responsible specialist; do not transfer approval to a different load, rack or tray revision. For produce crate cooling rather than tray-to-rail fit, see the [precooling guide](/resources/insights/260821-produce-crate-precooling-ventilation/).

## Put the accepted interface in the order

Send each supplier the same brief: equipment and rail drawing, measured engagement and vertical envelope, tray orientation, packaged goods and mass, temperature and dwell, required cleaning process, sample and evidence requirements, quantity and destination. Ask for the proposed model and revision, dimensions and tolerances, load and temperature conditions, support guidance and changes requiring reapproval. Agree how deliveries will be compared with the accepted sample.
