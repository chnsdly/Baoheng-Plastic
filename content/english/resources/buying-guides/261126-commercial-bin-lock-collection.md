A lock can restrict routine access yet prevent the collector from emptying the bin if no one can release it. **Agree who opens the lid, how the collector empties it and what happens when the lock fails before ordering the bin-and-lock combination.** The picture shows closed bins without a visible lock; it does not establish lock availability or compatibility.

## Choose the access and emptying sequence

Record the site, waste stream, bin model and lid, authorised users, collection service and equipment. A manually unlocked lock or padlock requires a named person and an agreed key or release procedure before collection. A gravity-release lock may release during the tipping action on a compatible installation, but its operation depends on the exact lock, orientation, bin and lifter. Confirm with the manufacturer and collector; a lock may also release if the bin is tilted outside collection. Do not assume automatic release is suitable for confidential material or every collection method.

[Weber's accessory information](https://www.w-weber.com/en/wheelie-bin-waste-container-options-accessories.html) describes padlock brackets for its own bins. [Franzen's automatic-lock information](https://e-handel.sansac.se/Media/Documents/Franzen-locks_2022.pdf) describes a particular automatic mechanism and bin-dependent mounting plate. Neither is a claim that Baoheng supplies or approves those locks. If an aperture permits disposal while the lid remains closed, check the accepted waste and access requirements; an aperture does not guarantee security.

## Freeze the fitting and the handoff

Ask for the exact bin, lid and lock revisions, mounting drawing, key system, release method, repair parts, inspection and cleaning instructions. Check that the mechanism does not obstruct the lid, handles, identification or lifting interface. Do not drill or improvise attachments without the bin and lock suppliers' approval. Confirm supply and commercial terms for each order.

Record who holds keys, who unlocks or presents the bin, who checks relocking, and the response to a lost key, jammed mechanism or changed vehicle. If a lock cannot be released as agreed, hold collection and contact the responsible parties rather than forcing it or bypassing safeguards.

## Test the complete service, not just the latch

With the site and collector, agree a safe sample and authorised trial. Observe normal deposit, closing, access by authorised users, collection presentation, release or removal, emptying, return and relocking using the actual approved equipment. Check for binding, damage, unplanned opening or interference. Stop if access, retention or collection cannot be controlled; record the accepted configuration and exclusions. Reassess changes to the lock, lid, collector, vehicle, key arrangement or site.

A lock does not replace a site risk assessment or the pre-collection check for people in large bins. UK [HSE guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/waste/preventing.htm) addresses access, siting and collection checks; use the applicable local requirements.
