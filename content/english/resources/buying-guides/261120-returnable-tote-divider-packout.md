A reusable tote that fits a component may still allow parts to rub, shift or arrive in the wrong orientation. Before a volume order, compare **the exact tote, internal packing, parts and return route as one system**. Choose loose packing only when the part owner accepts the expected contact; investigate dividers or fitted trays when separation, positioning or a sensitive surface matters.

The image shows an empty outer crate without an insert. It does not prove that a particular divider or custom insert is available from Baoheng.

## Define the part and contact to protect

Record the part drawing and revision, maximum dimensions including projections, mass, permitted orientation, sensitive surfaces, contamination limits and the approved points where packing may touch it. Note whether the part is oily, sharp, fragile or electrostatic-sensitive; special cleanliness or ESD requirements need their own confirmed evidence. Record fill and empty stations, gloves, cycle frequency, transport, temperature, cleaning and who approves the packaging.

Compare three starting options against those facts. **Loose parts** may be suitable where contact and sorting are acceptable. **A divider grid or interleaf** can separate parts but its edges, height and retention need checking. **A fitted tray or pocket** can locate a specific part but can reduce usable space or complicate cleaning and changeover. These are design options, not universal performance rankings. [ORBIS describes sheet, textile and foam dunnage](https://www.orbiscorporation.com/en-us/products/dunnage/) for its own applications; request evidence for the materials and construction actually offered.

## Estimate cells and loaded mass

First measure the tote's usable internal space at the actual fill height, not its outside footprint. In this **illustrative** layout, usable space is 560 × 360 mm and the planned cell pitches are 140 × 90 mm. If those pitches already include every divider and edge allowance, the calculation gives 4 × 4 = **16 cells** for one layer. If one part weighs 0.5 kg, parts weigh 16 × 0.5 = **8 kg**. If the tote and insert together weigh 2 kg, the loaded unit is **10 kg**. These inputs are assumptions, not product specifications or lifting limits.

A pitch is not the clear opening: subtract divider thickness and any required clearance, check the part's projections and grip, and verify the sample at the narrowest point. Allow for tapered walls, corner radii, fasteners and tolerances not already included; otherwise reduce the count. Additional layers need their own support and access check. Confirm filled mass, centre of gravity, stack and carrier limits with the responsible parties; arithmetic alone approves none of them.

## Run the real pack, unpack and return sequence

Agree the tote and insert revisions, representative parts, contact points, quantity, route, stop criteria and decision owners before testing. Load and remove the first, widest and last parts using normal gloves and work positions. Under the approved route, observe retention during handling, storage and transport; check part-to-part and part-to-divider contact, surface damage, insert movement, tote distortion, pinch points and any need to force a part.

Stop and hold the case if parts bind, shift out of position, show damage, the tote becomes unstable or the agreed safe handling method cannot be maintained. The site's responsible team assesses the task; [UK HSE guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/msd/manual-handling/assess-manual-handling.htm) identifies load, posture, frequency and work environment as relevant factors. Apply local requirements; no universal safe lifting weight follows from the example.

After unloading, check how the insert is identified, cleaned, inspected, nested or folded and returned with the empty tote. Record missing, damaged or contaminated pieces and who decides reuse. A fitted insert that protects the outbound part can still be unsuitable if it blocks the empty return or cannot be maintained.

## Put the accepted combination in the RFQ

Send the same part drawing, contact restrictions, quantity per tote, cell layout, loaded mass, route, empty-return arrangement, environment, cleaning and approval criteria to each supplier. Ask for the exact tote and insert revisions, usable dimensions, material and contact information, sample scope, replacement and change-notification arrangements. Confirm who provides the insert; its availability, design and cost are not assumed.
