Two collections a week can still leave a business with overflowing bins over the weekend. The cause may be the four-day gap between Thursday and Monday, even when the average weekly collection capacity looks generous.

**Size each waste stream for the busiest interval between collections. Divide that volume by the usable capacity of one accepted bin, then round up.** Check mass, access and the collection arrangement before ordering. Staff headcount and nominal litres alone cannot give a reliable bin count.

## Measure what accumulates before the truck arrives

For an operating site, record each waste stream separately over representative collection cycles. Include a busy period, delivery days and weekend activity where relevant. Note the bin size, fill level just before emptying, collection time, overflow and any extra removal. If a bin is already overflowing, its contents alone understate the amount produced.

Keep the waste in the condition in which it will actually be stored. Flattened cardboard and whole cartons occupy different volumes. An estimate based on flattened material is useful only if that preparation will happen consistently and the collector accepts it.

The [US EPA commercial-building guide](https://www.epa.gov/smm/managing-and-reducing-wastes-guide-commercial-buildings) recommends waste assessments to establish quantity and composition. For this purchase, use those observations to build a volume record for each collection interval. Have the responsible site team choose a safe measurement method; routine capacity checks do not require staff to climb into bins or handle unknown contents.

If the facility is new, start with comparable operations or a documented forecast and arrange a review after opening. Keep the assumptions visible instead of treating a per-person estimate as measured demand.

## Use the collection calendar, not a weekly average

Mark actual collection days and times. For Monday and Thursday collections at the same time of day, the gaps are three days and four days. A seven-day business generating waste throughout the week needs enough storage for the longer interval. For irregular production, add the expected volumes within each interval rather than multiplying one average day.

Repeat this separately for general waste, dry recycling, glass and food waste according to the collector's accepted streams. Spare room in a recycling bin cannot be counted as general-waste capacity when mixing is prohibited. The [EPA's commercial-bin guidance](https://www.epa.gov/transforming-waste-tool/commercial-bins) connects service capacity to both container size and collection frequency. It is a planning reference, not a universal requirement to buy equal numbers of bins.

Also mark holidays, seasonal peaks and the agreed response to a missed collection. More frequent service can reduce the routine bin count, but it creates a stronger dependency on collections arriving as planned.

## A worked comparison: 660 L or 1,100 L?

Assume one waste stream produces **180 L per day**, every day, with Monday and Thursday collections. The longest normal gap contains **720 L**. For this illustration, the site reserves **15% of nominal volume**, leaving an **85% usable share** to account for its observed filling pattern and lid closure. That allowance is an example chosen for the calculation, not a standard fill limit or a supplier rating.

Use:

**Usable capacity per bin = nominal volume × usable share**  
**Bins required = round up (volume in the planning interval ÷ usable capacity per bin)**

These counts assume the waste can reach one shared storage area. Calculate separate areas individually when capacity cannot be shared in practice.

Neither result includes other waste streams, cleaning downtime or a missed collection. If a Monday collection is missed and the next one is Thursday, the interval from the previous Thursday becomes seven days: **180 × 7 = 1,260 L**. Two 660 L bins provide **1,122 L usable**, and one 1,100 L bin provides **935 L usable**. Both routine arrangements would need an agreed contingency.

Resolve that gap with the collector: reserve storage, a confirmed extra collection or another permitted arrangement. Do not count bags beside the bins as capacity unless the service explicitly accepts them.

For your site, replace the daily volume and usable share with supported assumptions. Avoid adding the same allowance twice by inflating both the waste estimate and the headroom without explaining why.

## Let mass and the available space decide between the candidates

The calculation produces a shortlist. It does not establish which bin is practical.

Dense waste may reach the accepted mass limit while the bin still has empty space. Ask for the exact bin's documented load conditions and the collector's service limit, then check the representative filled condition. Litres cannot be converted to kilograms with one factor for every waste stream.

As a local service example, [Herefordshire Council](https://www.herefordshire.gov.uk/business-waste-service) requires closed lids, manageable bin weight and no waste compaction. Confirm your own collector's current rules before setting usable volume or planning to compress waste.

Draw the candidate bins in the actual storage area using their external dimensions. Include lid movement, user access and the space needed to move each bin to collection. A single larger bin may reduce the count while creating a problem at a doorway or on the handling route. Use the existing [bin push-route guide](/resources/buying-guides/260904-commercial-wheeled-bin-push-route-check/) for that assessment and the [lift-interface guide](/resources/buying-guides/260828-commercial-wheeled-bin-lift-interface-check/) for collector acceptance.

Compare complete service quotations for the same waste forecast: bin purchase or hire, scheduled lifts, extra collections, cleaning and any relevant excess-weight or rejected-load charges. A lower unit price does not tell you the ongoing cost of the arrangement.

## Buy a capacity plan that can be checked

Keep a short purchasing record for each stream: measured or forecast volume, busiest interval, usable-volume assumption, candidate size and count, mass limit, storage layout, collection schedule and missed-service response. Name who will review it after the first representative operating period.

[Belfast's commercial waste-storage guidance](https://www.belfastcity.gov.uk/documents/waste-storage-guidelines/commercial-developments-in-belfast) treats bin size, number and collection frequency as interdependent and warns that reduced storage increases exposure to missed collections. Its local planning scope matters; use your own authority's requirements for the site.
