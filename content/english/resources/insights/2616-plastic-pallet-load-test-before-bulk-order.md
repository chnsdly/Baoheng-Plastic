A catalogue load number is a starting point, not approval for every warehouse. Before a bulk order, identify the exact pallet configuration, describe the load and support conditions, review applicable test evidence, and agree what the site must verify. **A floor result does not establish a rack rating, and one successful trial does not establish a universal safety margin.**

*The cover is illustrative; it is not a test result, an approved setup or evidence for a particular model.*

## Describe the load case before comparing results

Record the model and drawing revision, material and reinforcement configuration, dimensions, intended quantity and destination. For the unit load, record the maximum mass including packaging, footprint, load distribution, concentrated contact points, overhang and restraint. Describe each required storage and handling route: floor or stack, rack type and support, beam span and orientation, forklift or pallet-jack entry, conveyor contacts, temperature, exposure and dwell time.

For example, cartons spread across a deck and drums resting on small contact areas can impose different conditions even at the same total mass. Ask the supplier whether the test load distribution and support arrangement represent each actual use. Treat materially different uses as separate cases; a missing match means more evidence is needed, not automatic rejection or approval.

## Review the report, not just the rating

Static, dynamic and rack ratings describe different situations. Ask how each is defined for the exact configuration. Obtain the method and report, sample identification, support positions and contact widths, load distribution, temperature, duration, measurement locations, result and stated limitations. If another material, reinforcement or base is proposed, ask whether the evidence still applies.

[ISO 8611-1:2025](https://www.iso.org/standard/87543.html) describes test methods for new flat pallets; it also notes the value of field tests for specific designs. A reference to the standard does not prove this model was tested to it. Ask for the actual applicable report; a site fit check does not by itself establish a rated capacity.

## Agree safe checks and stop rules first

Before a loaded trial, the responsible site team, supplier and relevant rack or equipment specialists should agree on the candidate, representative goods, equipment, support, temperature, duration, safe procedure, measurement points and acceptance limits. Do not place a loaded sample on beams merely to discover its limit. Confirm the configuration is suitable for that support before any loaded rack trial.

Start with an inspection and unloaded fit check. Under the approved procedure, observe required lifting, travel and placement with a representative load. Where relevant, record deflection at agreed points and times against the agreed limit, together with clearance, load stability, cracks and permanent distortion. Stop and isolate a sample if it cracks, becomes unstable, jams, loses support or exceeds an agreed limit. A qualified responsible person decides the next step; do not infer a new safe load from a stopped trial.

Select sample quantity and duration from the conditions and evidence needed, not a universal number. The [pilot guide](/resources/insights/2604-plastic-pallet-pilot-program/) covers trial ownership, route checks and go/modify/stop decisions.

## Tie the result to the order

Keep the approved model and revision, material and reinforcement, drawing, allowed use, load and support conditions, test report, observations, acceptance decision and approver with the order. Agree how delivered lots will be checked against the approved sample and how discrepancies will be handled.
